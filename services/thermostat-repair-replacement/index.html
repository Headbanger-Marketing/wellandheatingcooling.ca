Is the thermostat reading the room or only the air around itself?

A thermostat reads only the air that reaches its own sensor, so a spot near a register, lamp, sunny window, or exterior wall can mislead the equipment. Record where the control and any remote sensor sit before anyone proposes a replacement.

Assessment and Scope

Questions for the Proposed Work

01

Photograph the mounting spot

Record the wall the control sits on and photograph what surrounds it, such as a supply register, lamp, sunny window, or exterior wall. Add a line on how often that wall is in direct sun or shade.

02

Pair readings with a thermometer

Place a separate room thermometer away from the control and write both readings at the same times each day. A steady gap, or one that changes with sun or running equipment, shows whether placement matters.

03

Ask for the sensing explanation

Ask the contractor to state whether the sensor, the control, or its location explains the gap, and which test showed it. A replacement proposal should say why a new control would read differently in the same spot.

Compare the next step

Compare keeping the control where it is, having a qualified contractor relocate it, and replacing it, using the recorded gap between readings. A new control in the same spot may read the same, so the contractor should name the finding behind each option.

Equipment Observations to Record
